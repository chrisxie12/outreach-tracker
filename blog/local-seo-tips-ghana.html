Local SEO Tips for Small Businesses in Ghana

Aug 20267 min read

Picture this: someone in Osu pulls out their phone and searches "best restaurant near me." Another person in East Legon types "hair salon close by." A homeowner in Tema googles "plumber near me" because a pipe just burst. In every case, Google shows a handful of local businesses at the top of the results. If your business is not among them, you are invisible to the very people ready to pay you. Local SEO is how you get on that list — and it costs nothing but your time.

What is local SEO and why it matters in Ghana

Local SEO is the process of optimising your online presence so your business shows up when people nearby search for what you offer. Unlike general SEO, which targets broad keywords, local SEO focuses on location-based searches like "hair salon near me," "best restaurant in Osu," or "pharmacy in Tema." For Ghanaian small businesses, this is the single most powerful way to attract customers without paying for ads. Google processes millions of these "near me" searches every day, and the businesses that appear in the top three results — the so-called "local pack" — capture the vast majority of clicks and calls.

Real Ghanaian businesses winning with local SEO

Consider a hair salon in East Legon. When someone nearby searches "hair salon near me," the salon's Google Business Profile shows up with a 4.8-star rating, dozens of photos of styled hair, and a "Call Now" button. That listing alone brings in five to ten new walk-in customers every week — all without the salon spending a cedi on Facebook ads.

A pharmacy in Tema Community 7 took a similar approach. By claiming and fully optimising its Google Business Profile — adding accurate opening hours, a full list of services, and photos of the shop front — the pharmacy began appearing on Google Maps whenever anyone in Tema searched for a nearby chemist. Foot traffic increased noticeably within two months.

Even service businesses benefit. A plumbing company in Kumasi started including "plumber in Kumasi" and "emergency plumbing Kumasi" on its website pages. Within a few weeks, the business began receiving calls from homeowners searching "plumber near me" across the Kumasi metro area. No billboards. No radio ads. Just local search.

How to optimise your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the foundation of local SEO. It is the listing that appears on Google Search and Google Maps when someone searches for your business type in your area. Here is how to make it work for you:

  • Claim and verify your profile. Go to google.com/business, search for your business, and follow the verification steps. Google will send you a postcard with a PIN, or you may be able to verify by phone or email.
  • Fill out every field. Add your exact business name, full address, phone number, website, business hours, service areas, and a detailed description of what you do. Profiles that are 100% complete rank significantly higher than half-finished ones.
  • Choose the right categories. Pick a primary category that matches your core business (e.g., "Hair Salon," "Pharmacy," "Plumber") and add secondary categories where relevant.
  • Add high-quality photos. Upload photos of your storefront, interior, products, and team. Businesses with photos receive 42% more direction requests and 35% more click-throughs than those without.
  • Post weekly updates. Use GBP's post feature to share offers, events, or news. Google rewards active profiles with better visibility.

Keep your NAP consistent everywhere

NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone number. This information must be identical across every place your business appears online — your website, Google Business Profile, Facebook page, Instagram bio, and any Ghanaian business directories. Even small inconsistencies like "14 Independence Ave" versus "No. 14 Independence Avenue" or using a different phone number can confuse Google and push your listing down in results. Pick one format and use it everywhere.

Get and respond to customer reviews

Reviews are one of the strongest ranking signals for local SEO. Google trusts businesses that other people vouch for. Ask every satisfied customer to leave a review on your Google Business Profile. You can share a direct review link via WhatsApp or text message to make it easy. More importantly, respond to every review — thank people for positive feedback and address complaints professionally. A business that engages with its reviews signals trustworthiness and activity to Google.

Use local keywords on your website

If you have a website, include your city or neighbourhood name in your page titles, headings, and body text. For example, a restaurant in Osu might use a page title like "Best Restaurant in Osu, Accra | [Restaurant Name]." A plumbing service in Kumasi should naturally mention "Kumasi" and surrounding areas in its service descriptions. This helps Google understand exactly where you operate and match you to local searches.

Optimise for Google Maps

Google Maps is where most "near me" searches land. To optimise for Maps, make sure your Google Business Profile is fully complete, add your service area, embed a Google Map on your contact page, and ensure your address matches what is registered with Google. Encourage reviews that mention your location — for instance, "Great salon in East Legon!" — because Google uses review text to understand your relevance to specific areas.

Local SEO Checklist

Use this checklist to get started today:

  • Claim and verify your Google Business Profile at google.com/business
  • Complete every section of your profile — name, address, phone, hours, description, categories
  • Upload at least 10 high-quality photos of your business, products, or team
  • Ensure your NAP (Name, Address, Phone) is identical on your website, GBP, and all social media profiles
  • Ask your five most recent happy customers to leave a Google review this week
  • Respond to every review you have received — positive or negative
  • Add your city or neighbourhood to your website's page titles and headings
  • Post a weekly update or offer on your Google Business Profile to stay active

Frequently asked questions

What is local SEO?

Local SEO is the practice of optimising your online presence — especially your Google Business Profile and website — so your business appears in search results when people nearby look for the products or services you offer. It is different from general SEO because it targets location-specific queries like "hair salon near me" or "best restaurant in Osu."

How do I rank on Google Maps?

To rank on Google Maps, claim and fully optimise your Google Business Profile with accurate information, photos, and regular posts. Get consistent reviews from customers, keep your NAP consistent across all platforms, and ensure your address is registered correctly with Google. The more complete and active your profile is, the higher you will appear in the local map results.

Do I need a website for local SEO?

A website is not strictly required — a fully optimised Google Business Profile alone can drive significant local traffic. However, a website strengthens your local SEO by giving you more pages to target local keywords, showcase your services, and build credibility. Even a simple one-page site is better than no site at all.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number, but more positive reviews generally mean better rankings and more clicks. Focus on consistency rather than volume — aim to get a few new reviews every month. Respond to all of them. Google also looks at your review velocity (how quickly you accumulate them) and the diversity of reviewers, so steady growth matters more than a sudden spike.

Local SEO is not a one-time setup. It requires consistent effort — updating your profile, gathering reviews, posting content, and keeping your information accurate. But for small businesses in Ghana, it is the most cost-effective way to get found by customers who are actively searching for what you offer. Start with the checklist above, take it one step at a time, and watch your business appear where it matters most: right at the top of local search results.
